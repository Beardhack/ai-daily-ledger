# AI Daily Ledger style guide

Write for engineers and scientists who want a sourced account of what was said, what evidence supports it, and what remains uncertain. Favor concrete nouns, ordinary verbs, short causal explanations, and direct attribution.

This guide incorporates the useful warnings in Wikipedia's [Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing). That page is a descriptive field guide, not a prescriptive authority. Its value here is as an editing checklist for habits that have made recent editions sound formulaic.

## State the point directly

- Avoid stock negative parallelism: “not X, but Y,” “not just X,” “X rather than Y,” and stacked “no X, no Y, just Z” constructions. Use one only when a real distinction is central to the source. Do not use it as paragraph rhythm.
- Do not invent a misconception for the article to correct. Begin with the observed claim, result, failure, or decision.
- Prefer plain `is`, `are`, and `has` when they are accurate. Do not replace them mechanically with `serves as`, `features`, `offers`, `represents`, or similar inflated verbs.

## Keep analysis specific

- Name the person, company, paper, benchmark, or dataset behind every material claim. Avoid `experts say`, `observers note`, `industry reports`, and other vague authorities.
- Explain a mechanism only when the post or established technical knowledge supports it. Separate inference from reported fact.
- Cut generic significance claims such as `marks a shift`, `underscores the importance`, `reflects a broader trend`, `changing landscape`, and `pivotal moment`. State the concrete consequence instead.
- Cut trailing participial glosses that merely decorate a fact: `highlighting`, `underscoring`, `ensuring`, `reflecting`, `fostering`, and `contributing to`.
- Do not end each section with a sweeping lesson. End when the evidence and its implications have been covered.

## Let the prose breathe

- Do not force claims, examples, caveats, or adjectives into groups of three. Use the number the evidence requires.
- Repetition is acceptable when it preserves precision. Do not cycle through synonyms to create artificial variety.
- Use em dashes sparingly. Prefer a period, comma, or colon when the relationship is straightforward.
- Keep headings concrete and in sentence case. Avoid slogans that could label many unrelated AI stories.
- Avoid canned correspondence and stage directions: `You're right`, `Of course`, `Here is`, `In this section`, `The practical lesson is`, and `I hope this helps`.

## Prepublication style pass

Read the whole draft aloud once. Search for the constructions and vocabulary above. Rewrite formulaic passages instead of swapping in synonyms. Preserve any construction that is needed for accuracy or appears inside a clearly attributed quotation.

The final draft should sound like a reporter who understands the engineering: precise about provenance, comfortable with simple sentences, and willing to stop without manufacturing a flourish.
